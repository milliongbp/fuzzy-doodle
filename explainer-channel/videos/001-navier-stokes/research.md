# Navier-Stokes: The Equations That Control Everything (But We Don't Fully Understand Them)

**picked:** 2026-09-11 · score D5/S5/V4/E5/R5 = 24/25 · reason: OpenAI breakthrough (Sept 2026) proves smooth fluids can develop singularities; explains a 200-year-old paradox: we use these equations for weather/aircraft but can't prove they work forever.

## Facts (script may only use these)

- Navier-Stokes equations were derived independently by Claude-Louis Navier (France) and George Gabriel Stokes (England) in the early 19th century — [Going with the Flow, Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/science/going-with-the-flow-how-george-gabriel-stokes-s-influence-still-resonates-1.3963814)
- The equations represent Newton's second law of motion applied to fluids — they describe how velocity, pressure, temperature, and density of a moving fluid are related — [COMSOL](https://www.comsol.com/multiphysics/navier-stokes-equations)
- They govern weather forecasting: numerical models of Navier-Stokes are the basis for all modern weather forecasts, though accuracy is limited by the chaotic nature of weather and uncertainty in initial conditions — [STRUCTURES Blog, Uni Heidelberg](https://structures.uni-heidelberg.de/blog/posts/2025_02/index.php)
- Aircraft design: aerospace engineers use Navier-Stokes (in approximate form) to understand airflow over wings and predict aerodynamic efficiency — [Examples.com](https://www.examples.com/physics/navier-stokes-equations.html)
- Climate modeling: Navier-Stokes equations underpin global climate models predicting warming from greenhouse gases; unlike weather models, climate models can compute for longer periods by tuning to historical data — [IEEE Technology Navigator](https://technav.ieee.org/topic/navier-stokes-equations/)
- The Millennium Prize Problem: The Clay Mathematics Institute offers $1 million for proving either that every smooth 3D incompressible fluid stays smooth forever (existence and smoothness), or finding a counterexample showing smooth fluids can develop singularities (blow up) in finite time — [Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/navier-stokes-equation/)
- **OpenAI September 2026 breakthrough**: OpenAI announced a resolution using AI proving that an initially smooth fluid at rest, driven by a smooth force, can develop a singularity in finite time — this establishes the "blowup counterexample" case, disproving global smoothness — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02842-5)
- The paradox: we've used Navier-Stokes for ~100 years in engineering and weather prediction despite not knowing if the math fundamentally works forever — [200 Years of Navier-Stokes, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.13669)
